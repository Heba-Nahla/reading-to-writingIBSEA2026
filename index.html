Learning intentions

  1. Identify opportunities to integrate reading, writing and communication into meaningful inquiry.
  2. Explore how homeroom and language teachers can co-support literacy within Units of Inquiry.
  3. Design a learning experience that connects inquiry, literacy and creative expression.
  4. Identify opportunities for student voice, choice and ownership.
  5. Select digital or non-digital tools based on learning intentions.
  6. Identify meaningful evidence of learning through feedback, assessment and reflection.

Two lenses, one learner

Homeroom lens: conceptual understanding, transdisciplinary themes, inquiry that leads to action.

Language lens: vocabulary and structures, genre, purpose and audience, targeted feedback on writing.

Together: concepts give language a purpose, and language gives concepts a voice.

The Voice Pathway

Five steps from reading to writing. Learner agency (voice, choice and ownership) runs through every step.

Worked example · Sharing the planet

Central idea: Living things depend on each other and on their environment.

Lines of inquiry: how living things depend on one another · how human choices change habitats · our responsibility to protect living things.

Key concepts: Connection, Causation, Responsibility · Learner profile: Caring, Communicators · ATL: Thinking, Communication, Research

Investigate: Read beyond the words

Mentor text

The Fig Tree

For forty years, the old fig tree stood beside the school wall. Sparrows built nests in its arms, and children ate their lunch in its cool shade.

On Monday morning, a yellow machine rumbled through the gate. By Friday, only a stump remained.

On the empty wall, a small bird sat very still, looking at the place where its home used to be.

An original short text written for this workshop. You may reuse and adapt it freely.

Four ways to read beyond the words

  • See · Think · Wonder: a visible-thinking routine that slows reading down.
  • Vocabulary detectives: collect powerful words; order them on a word cline.
  • Question the author: Whose voice is missing? What does the author want us to feel?
  • Change the lens: retell one moment through another character’s eyes.

Word cline builder

Add three to five words and arrange them from weakest to strongest (e.g. sad → upset → heartbroken).

    Compose: the story through the bird’s eyes

    Talk it → word bank → sentence frames → storyboard → draft & TAG. Sketch in each panel (finger, stylus or mouse) and add a caption, or print a blank copy for paper.

    Sentence frames: From my branch, I could see… · I felt ___ because ___ . · If I could speak to the people, I would say…
    Success criteria: ✓ clear beginning, middle and end · ✓ a feeling and a reason · ✓ a message for a real audience

    Reimagine: the digital toolbox

    One text, multiple voices. Choose the mode first (speak it, draw it, move it, publish it), then the tool. Before using any tool with students, check your school’s data-protection policy, the platform’s current terms and its age requirements.

    Tool chooser: purpose before platform

    Start with what your learners need to do. Every digital option has a non-digital twin, so no learner is excluded.

    Publish & reflect

    TAG feedback

    T · Tell something you like

    A · Ask a question you wonder about

    G · Give a kind, specific suggestion

    3 · 2 · 1 reflection

    Voice

    Learners choose the message they want the audience to hear.

    Choice

    Learners choose the mode: speak, draw, move or publish.

    Ownership

    Learners co-create success criteria and judge their own drafts.

    Evidence of learning

    Monitor: observe talk in reading roles; listen for perspective language. Document: storyboards, drafts, recordings, VoiceThread comments. Measure: a single-point rubric based on co-created success criteria.
